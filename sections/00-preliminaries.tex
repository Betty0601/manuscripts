\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section fixes the preliminary definitions and notation used throughout
the manuscript.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{subsec:notation}

We write $\lgN$, $\lgZ$, $\lgQ$, $\lgR$, and $\lgC$ for the natural, integer,
rational, real, and complex numbers, and $\lgF{q}$ for the finite field with
$q$ elements. Quantum state vectors use Dirac notation $\lgket{\cdot}$ and
$\lgbra{\cdot}$, following the conventions of
Nielsen~\&~Chuang~\cite{NielsenChuang2010}.

\subsection{Mathematical background}
\label{subsec:math-background}

We assume elementary linear algebra over $\lgR$ and $\lgC$, probability
distributions on finite sets, and the basic notions of modular arithmetic in
$\lgZ/n\lgZ$.

\subsection{Cryptographic primitives}
\label{subsec:crypto-primitives}

A public-key encryption scheme, a key-encapsulation mechanism (KEM), and a
digital signature scheme each consist of a triple of probabilistic
polynomial-time algorithms; their standard indistinguishability and
unforgeability security notions are recalled as needed, following the
treatment of Katz~\&~Lindell~\cite{KatzLindell2020}. The lattice-based
constructions targeted in this training project, ML-KEM and ML-DSA, operate
over module lattices; the survey by Peikert~\cite{Peikert2016} gives an
overview of the area.
